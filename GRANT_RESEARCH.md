# Grant funding for CCSTA — research notes

Compiled 22 August 2026, revised same day. Every program below changes its
rules annually. Confirm directly with the funder before building anything
around it.

---

## The two questions that decide everything

### 1. Is CCSTA a "member-funded society" in the legal sense?

**This is almost certainly not what it sounds like, and the distinction
matters enormously.**

Being funded by member schools does *not* make an organisation a member-funded
society. That phrase is a formal designation under the BC Societies Act, and a
society only has it if it deliberately took specific steps to get it:

- a member-funded statement written into the constitution,
- approved by the members by special resolution,
- and, for a society that already existed before the current Act, a **court
  order** declaring it isn't prohibited from including that statement.

No society acquires this by accident, and a pre-existing society certainly
doesn't acquire it without someone having gone to court for it.

Two further tests make it unlikely to apply to CCSTA even if someone had tried:

- A member-funded society **cannot be a registered charity.**
- A society is disqualified from the designation if, over the preceding two
  financial years, it received more than $20,000 in public donations or
  government funding *and* that exceeded 10% of gross income.

And decisively: **a member-funded society cannot receive government funding at
all.** So this isn't one eligibility rule among many — if CCSTA somehow is one,
every government program on this list is closed by definition. If it isn't
one (the likely case), this exclusion simply doesn't apply.

**How to check:** CCSTA's constitution, or its BC Registries filing. A
member-funded society must say so explicitly in the constitution. If that
statement isn't there, CCSTA isn't one. This takes minutes, not weeks, and it
should be the first thing anyone does.

### 2. Does the schools exclusion catch us?

**This is the real open question, and it isn't resolved.**

Community Gaming Grants exclude "educational institutions, schools or school
authorities." CCSTA is not a school — it's a transportation association — so
there's a reasonable argument it isn't caught. But it exists to serve schools
and is funded by them, and only the Gaming Grants branch can say how they read
that.

They answer eligibility questions before you apply. One phone call describing
the organisation plainly settles this, in either direction, and saves months.

### On the religious affiliation — not an issue, but it doesn't rescue anything

Worth stating precisely, because it's easy to hear this the wrong way. BC's
guidelines say non-profits **affiliated with a religious institution are
eligible**. What's ineligible is a *program* whose purpose is the advancement
of religion. Busing children to school isn't that, so CCSTA clears this test.

But clearing it doesn't help with the other two. These are separate,
independent requirements, and an organisation has to pass all of them. Passing
the religion test is simply one hurdle that isn't in our way — it doesn't
offset the schools question, which remains genuinely unanswered.

## What's already dead — don't spend time here

**Canada Digital Adoption Program (CDAP).** The program most people will
recommend, and it no longer exists. "Grow Your Business Online" closed
September 2024; the program formally ended 31 March 2025, with no replacement
announced. Anyone suggesting CDAP is working from stale information.

## Provincial funding — the largest amounts

### BC Community Gaming Grants

Roughly $140M a year to BC non-profits. Two streams are relevant, subject
entirely to question 2 above.

**Capital Project Grants.** The closest fit to what CCSTA needs — the stated
purpose explicitly includes "update technology and purchase new equipment,"
which is unusually direct. Most funders make you argue that technology counts.
$5M annual pool.

> **2026 intake: 17 July – 31 August.** Nine days from today. Realistically too
> tight for a first-time application, but the date is worth diarising for July
> 2027.

**Program Grants.** Up to $125,000/year for local organisations, $250,000/year
for regional or provincial ones. Intake is sector-specific, which is what
catches out first-time applicants:

| Sector | 2026 window |
|---|---|
| Arts and culture | 1 Feb – 30 Apr |
| Sport | 1 Mar – 31 May |
| PAC / DPAC | 1 Apr – 30 Jun |
| Environment, public safety | 1 Jul – 31 Aug |
| **Human and social services** | **1 Aug – 30 Nov** ← open now |

Human and social services is the plausible category for CCSTA, and it runs to
the end of November — the one substantial window still reachable this year.

- [2026 Program Grant Guidelines (PDF)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/sports-recreation-arts-and-culture/gambling/grants/guide-cgg.pdf) — read the eligibility section first
- [Capital Project Grants](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/sports-culture/gambling-fundraising/gaming-grants/capital-project-grants)

### A route worth knowing about: the member schools themselves

If the schools exclusion does shut CCSTA out, the schools CCSTA serves may have
funding routes CCSTA doesn't. **PAC and DPAC grants are available to parent
advisory councils at public, Group 1 independent, and First Nation schools** —
which covers most independent Christian schools in BC.

That doesn't fund CCSTA directly, but transportation is a genuine parent-facing
cost, and a coordinated approach across several member schools' PACs may reach
money that a central application can't. Worth raising with the member schools
rather than treating CCSTA as the only possible applicant.

### Vancouver Foundation

BC's largest community foundation. The **Transforming Systems Grant** funds
systemic-change work on a multi-year operating basis and can absorb digital
infrastructure. Their other main streams (LEVEL BIPOC, Thriving Indigenous
Systems) target specific communities and won't fit CCSTA. Most streams require
registered charity or qualified-donee status — worth confirming CCSTA's status
before investing time here.

Vancouver Foundation publishes a full grant calendar each January.

### United Way BC

Several streams with capacity-building folded in. Digital infrastructure often
qualifies where it supports delivery of a funded program. Generally requires
registered charity status.

## Non-grant support — lower barrier, available now

Not cash, but real cost reduction, and mostly without application windows. If
the grant picture closes, this is where actual savings are.

**TechSoup Canada** — the main clearinghouse for donated and discounted software
for Canadian non-profits. Important detail: eligibility turns on **non-profit
incorporation**, not charitable registration, so a BC society incorporated
provincially can qualify without being a registered charity. Microsoft
licensing, Adobe, security software.
[Eligibility criteria](https://www.techsoup.ca/eligibility)

**Microsoft for Nonprofits** — free or discounted Microsoft 365 licences and
Azure credits. Directly relevant given Simply Accounting runs on Windows.

**Corporate community investment** — RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, Telus, BC Hydro
and FortisBC all run BC community programs, many with capacity-building streams
that fund technology tied to a clear community need. Smaller amounts, faster
decisions, far less paperwork than provincial or federal grants, and their
eligibility rules are generally looser than government programs.

**Google Ad Grants** — up to $10,000 USD/month in free search advertising, but
registered charities only, and CCSTA isn't trying to attract search traffic.
Listed so it can be ruled out deliberately rather than rediscovered later.

## How digital projects actually get funded

This matters more than the list above, because it separates applications that
get read from ones that don't.

**Funders don't fund software. They fund outcomes that happen to require it.**

- ✗ "We need to upgrade our booking system."
- ✓ "We've replaced a manual quoting process so member schools receive accurate
  trip costs in a day rather than a week, and so families aren't asked to cover
  shortfalls caused by trips that were under-quoted."

The second version is true of what CCSTA built this summer, which is a real
advantage — the work exists and its effects can be measured rather than
promised.

What reviewers consistently look for:

- **A real written quote from a real vendor.** An internal estimate is among the
  most common rejection reasons. A free consultation produces exactly this
  document, at no cost, whether or not the engagement proceeds.
- **Measurable outcomes** — quoting turnaround, dispatch hours saved, accuracy
  of trip costs, number of schools and students served.
- **Co-funding.** A project funded entirely by one grant reads as risky.
- **A sustainability plan** — who maintains this afterwards, and at what annual
  cost. Program officers have funded systems that died within 18 months and
  now ask directly.
- **Budget matching scope.** If the narrative describes integrations and the
  budget says $3,000, the reviewer assumes we don't know what it costs.

## Suggested order of operations

1. **Check the constitution** for a member-funded statement. Minutes of work,
   and it removes the question entirely one way or the other.
2. **Phone the Community Gaming Grants branch** and describe CCSTA plainly —
   a transportation association funded by member schools, busing students. Ask
   directly whether the schools exclusion applies. One call, either answer
   useful.
3. **If eligible** — target the Human and Social Services window (open to 30
   November) and diarise Capital Projects for July 2027.
4. **Regardless of the answer** — TechSoup Canada and Microsoft for Nonprofits
   have no meaningful barrier and can be actioned this month.
5. **Get a written quote** before drafting any application, not after.
